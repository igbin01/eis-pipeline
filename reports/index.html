Architecture

Seven modules under src/eispipe/, one test file per module.

ModuleResponsibility
spectrum.pyThe shared data type — Spectrum / SpectrumSeries — carrying frequency, impedance, and metadata (sample, immersion time, area, electrolyte) together.
io.pyLoading CSV and vendor instrument exports; manifest- or filename-pattern-driven time-series assembly; frequency cropping and inductive-point removal.
validate.pyKramers-Kronig linearity check, via impedance.py's Lin-KK implementation.
models.pyNamed equivalent-circuit definitions — intact_coating and degraded_coating — with data-driven initial-guess heuristics.
fit.pyCircuit fitting and parameter extraction — FitResult / FitSeries.
metrics.pyCPE→capacitance conversion (Hsu–Mansfeld), Brasher–Kingsbury water uptake, pore resistance, breakpoint frequency.
plots.pyNyquist, Bode, and metric-vs-time plotting.

Design decisions

Choices made deliberately, each with a specific reason rather than a library default.

  1. Nested circuit topology — R0-p(CPE1,R1-p(CPE2,R2)), not the more common series form. The substrate charge-transfer process is only electrochemically reachable through the coating pores, so it belongs inside the pore-resistance branch, not beside it.
  2. Kramers-Kronig failures are flagged, never discarded. A spectrum that fails linearity during a slow sweep is often the most informative one — it means the coating changed state mid-measurement.
  3. seed_from_previous defaults to False. Seeding each fit from the previous result produces smooth-looking parameter trajectories that can be an artifact of one bad earlier fit rather than real physics.
  4. Sign convention is auto-detected from column headers, with a data-driven sanity warning — never a silent correction — if most impedance points look inductive. Catches the common case of an unlabeled −Im(Z) column.

Bugs found and fixed

Each caught by actually exercising the code — a formula check, a full run — not by the test suite alone.

  1. drop_inductive vs. its own docstring. Claimed equivalence to impedance.py's ignoreBelowX, but implemented pointwise filtering instead of truncation from the first non-capacitive point. Corrected the docstring and added a test for the case where the two actually diverge.
  2. Sign-convention warning was written but never called. Existed as a function, wasn't wired into load_csv or load_instrument. Would have loaded reversed-sign data silently.
  3. FitResult.predict depended on dict insertion order matching circuit parameter order, with nothing enforcing it. A future reordering of the params dict would have silently swapped parameters. Fixed to index explicitly by param_names.
  4. RMSE reported in raw ohms while the fit itself is modulus-weighted — made degradation-over-time comparisons misleading, since |Z| falls by orders of magnitude over a series. Added a normalized chi2_norm figure of merit.
  5. _guess_degraded's initial-guess heuristic conflated R_po and R_ct when the two are well separated — the realistic case for a coating, where the two resistances routinely span orders of magnitude. Found on the first real end-to-end demo run, not by any unit test written beforehand.

Validation against published data

cpe_to_capacitance (Hsu–Mansfeld) checked against Policastro et al., Materials 2023, 16, 3623 (NRL, epoxy/polyurethane coating, 0.01 M NaCl, 5 °C, 3192 h) — their own fitted Rpo, Y0, n fed into our implementation of their Equation 2. This validates the capacitance formula specifically, not yet the fitting process itself, since Rpo/Y0/n were taken from the paper rather than produced by fit_series on digitized raw data.
